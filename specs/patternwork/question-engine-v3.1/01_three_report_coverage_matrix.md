# Patternwork Question Engine v3.1 — Three-Report Coverage Matrix

Release package: `3.1.0`  
Contract: `PWQE3-CONTRACT-2`  
Integrity contract: `PWQE3-INTEGRITY-1`

## Matrix semantics

Each row is one addressable coverage cell, identified as `CV-{report-section-code}`. The scoring engine updates the cell from attributable evidence objects; a bank item can nominate a section but cannot by itself mark it covered. Section confidence is `high`, `medium`, `low`, or `unsupported`. Routing colors are separate operational states:

- **Green:** section confidence is high or medium and all section-specific floors are met.
- **Amber:** low confidence, an unresolved `cap_low` contradiction, or an intentional limit accepted at stop. The cell records the exact limitation and missing evidence.
- **Red:** unsupported and required for the applicable report; route for evidence when burden permits.
- **Not applicable:** direct section-level inapplicability was established, with the reason and evidence ID retained. This is not missing evidence.

Route or domain absence is not coverage `not_applicable`: it merely disables an unavailable prompt route. `skipped`, `unknown`, inaccessible memory, and not-yet-asked are likewise not N/A and cannot satisfy a section floor. Coverage replication counts independent episode IDs under the packet snapshot/window binding; repeated fields from one episode do not increase the count.

Evidence grades (`direct_single`, `replicated`, `user_confirmed`, `cross_context`, `contradicted`) are independent tags. `Replicated` requires separate episodes rather than multiple fields from one episode. `Cross-context` requires distinct referents or life domains. A contradiction never disappears because supportive evidence is more numerous; it is resolved by a discriminator or preserved.

## Shared sufficiency rules

1. Scope/limits and confidence sections draw from packet metadata, applicability, provenance, contradictions, and coverage—not psychological inference.
2. A major part dossier normally requires two independent episode anchors and user identity confirmation. Without identity confirmation it remains a pattern cluster.
3. A state signature normally requires body/signature mapping in separate episodes plus at least one entry, exit, transition, or recovery sequence. A single sensation cannot classify a state.
4. An attachment sequence normally requires multiple cues or explicit user confirmation. It remains referent-specific unless cross-context evidence exists.
5. Safety/reliability context is a prerequisite for relational interpretation. In unsafe, coercive, threatening, or currently unstable contexts, the writer describes context-linked responses without trait or pathology language.
6. Quotes require typed, edited, or explicitly confirmed wording and an attributable raw-response ID. Selecting a prewritten option is not quote provenance.
7. No cell is Green solely from inferred signals. At least one direct or user-confirmed anchor is required.

## Routing use

After each interaction, the engine recomputes every applicable cell using attributable evidence IDs and records: current confidence, routing color, satisfied fields, missing fields, independent episode IDs/count, context count, open contradiction IDs with typed `report_impact`, and eligible next families. Candidate next interactions are ranked by expected Red/Amber coverage gain, uncertainty reduction, replication/cross-context need, burden, arousal, recent family repetition, and likelihood of concrete recall. A Green cell may reopen to Amber when a new `cap_low` contradiction appears.

The engine may stop only when every applicable required cell is Green or intentionally Amber with an explicit limitation, all candidate dispositions and major contradictions are resolved or retained, signature and sequence floors are met, final fit is complete, and expected coverage gain no longer justifies burden. Early stopping produces only supported prose and a machine-readable list of missing evidence; it never triggers generic filler.
