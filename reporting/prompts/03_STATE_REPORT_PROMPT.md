# State / polyvagal-informed report — v7

Use the v7 shared contract. `report_type = PV`.

Explain what changes in lived state and what those changes make easier or harder. Use attention, words, thought, energy, urge, sensation, available choices, and social contact. Organize by the actual movement and contrasts, not by filling autonomic categories. No required mobilized, low-energy, or settled state, and no requirement for a dramatic transition.

Give state descriptions usable names only when the handle helps. A 'narrowing' or 'low-on-words' description can be more accurate than a branded state. Use polyvagal language when it adds understanding, with an explicit model frame: mobilization, settling/social engagement, shutdown-like withdrawal, and mixed experiences may be useful descriptions. Do not infer a particular vagal circuit because the name sounds physiological. No vagal-tone estimate, measured baseline, compulsory hierarchical ladder, or claim that fawn is a separate autonomic branch. Intensity and bodily location do not determine autonomic mechanism.

Preserve channel differences: the person may continue working while feeling keyed up, want to answer while being unable to find words, or feel connected while needing less sensory input. These are differences between channels—not automatically the mind overriding the body, deliberate self-betrayal, dissociation, or concealed distress. The interpretation requires its own support.

Describe state-dependent meaning or thought only from linked evidence. Do not populate a low-energy state with hopelessness, a mobilized state with fear, or a settled state with openness by definition. Excitement and effective action are not inherently dysregulated. Quietness and reduced activity can be rest, constraint, choice, or limited capacity.

Map transitions only where timing is supplied: first noticed change, subsequent move, immediate effect, later capacity, and return of contact may differ. Noticing is not causal precedence. Effect and cause are separate. A generic ladder must not replace uncertainty, overlap, or reversal. No invented times of day, durations, breaths, body regions, sleeping patterns, or whole-day cycles.

Assess the repertoire visible in the sample. Which capacities stayed available? Which became harder? What returned? Do not infer a globally narrow nervous-system range from a sparse or stress-heavy packet. A missing recovery episode is not failed recovery. Distinguish ability to continue, relief, regained choice, settled feeling, and durability when demands return.

Describe self-regulation and co-regulation as observed experiences: what reduced input, movement, practical help, information, warmth, or someone's non-demanding presence changed. Do not transform association into a universal remedy. Company that increases effort or exposure is not necessarily helpful; solitude is not necessarily withdrawal. Retain the person's practical and relational context.

Do not label a threat interpretation a false alarm without relevant evidence about actual conditions. Symptoms mentioned in the packet remain descriptions, not proof of a psychological cause. Body preference affects presentation and available detail; declining sensations does not mean poor interoception.

Conclude with the distinctions or conditions that make this pattern easier to recognize. No required insight count, final revelation, capacity quota, nervous-system reset, breathing protocol, or treatment plan.
