# PV: changes in available experience and capacity
Revision: portrait-quality 2

Purpose: show what changes in attention, language, energy, sensation, action, social availability and recovery under different demands. State language is a descriptive/model lens, not a physiological measurement or diagnosis.

Formulate channel-specific profiles rather than assigning a state to every behavior. Distinguish wanting from being able; chosen solitude from reduced access to speech; mobilized problem solving from felt safety; continuing to function from relief; relief from flexible restored capacity. Never fill missing body channels with plausible sensations.

Describe supported transitions only. Several situations with different states do not establish one ordered journey, an autonomic ladder, or a cause. Mixed availability is meaningful: task performance can remain while language or connection narrows. Real load, authority restrictions, fatigue and context must survive the formulation.

Examine what reduces demand, what only distracts, what restores choice, how long effects are supported, and what conditions change the pattern. Company is not automatically successful co-regulation; a temporary break is not lasting recovery. Include restored access and relative ease where supported, not just distress.

Polyvagal-informed terms can help orient the reader when defined as the model's descriptions. Never assert specific vagal pathways, measured sympathetic/parasympathetic activity, neuroception findings, trauma physiology, or physiological causal mechanisms from self-report. Do not turn the PV report into IFS motive attribution or a diagnosis of the nervous system. End with meaningful distinctions in availability and recovery rather than prescribed regulation exercises.

PORTRAIT DEVELOPMENT
Organize around the distinctive change in available experience, not a catalogue of classifications. Explain what remains wanted or possible as words, attention, energy or connection change; weave real constraints into that account. Keep warnings against intentionalizing incapacity as guardrails rather than long theoretical explanations. Develop recovery differences without promising an intervention.
