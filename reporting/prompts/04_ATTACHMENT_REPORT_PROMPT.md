# Attachment report — v7

Use the v7 shared contract. `report_type = ATT`.

Describe how connection, separateness, dependence, uncertainty, receiving care, and repair work in the relationships and situations actually sampled. Let the account contain similarities as well as differences. Do not manufacture a partner/friend/family triptych, romantic material for someone without it, or a universal style from selective scenarios.

Use attachment anxiety, avoidance/deactivation, hyperactivating pursuit/monitoring, and secure functioning when they clarify supported patterns. Terms are neither forbidden nor required, and need not be restricted to a single token occurrence. Introduce them in ordinary language and keep the lived description primary. A cross-relationship tendency can be stated within the relationships sampled when supported by relevant comparisons. It is not a validated global attachment score or an immutable identity.

Working models are current expectations: what the person anticipates about another person's availability, their own acceptability, the cost of needing, or the prospect of repair. Infer a present organizing expectation when converging evidence supports it, distinguishing inferred formulation from direct selection. Do not infer childhood parenting, attachment injury, or a learned family template from that expectation.

Keep information-seeking, reassurance-seeking, expression of hurt, attempts to restore connection, and attempts to end internal tension distinct. Not all reaching is anxious, and not all distancing is avoidant. Rest, privacy, real unreliability, lower interest, practical stakes, and a justified boundary remain available explanations. Reported unreliability is the respondent's context, not access to the other person's mind.

Examine sequences and changes of goal: what the person does initially, what an unanswered move changes, and what the next move accomplishes. Do not call every sequence protest or force collapse at its end. Approach–withdrawal is not by itself evidence of disorganized attachment, fearful-avoidant identity, or unresolved trauma. Different assessment traditions are not interchangeable.

Preserve distinctions among asking for help, letting help happen, offering repair, receiving repair, immediate reassurance, reassurance durability, and later contact. A refusal of inadequate repair is not necessarily an inability to receive love. An asymmetry matters only as far as the sampled episodes make a meaningful comparison; no asymmetry found is not proof of perfect balance.

Relational pace includes privately developing importance, publicly shown interest, readiness to disclose, and willingness to reconnect after rupture. D52/D53 can support an internal/external pace difference in that connection; they do not establish lifetime bonding rarity, ideal speed, an exact threshold, or hidden intensity. Use the actual available detail, not a full pace profile template.

Name secure functioning where meaningful: realistic reliance, direct needs, separateness without retaliation, credible repair, or continued responsiveness under tension. These are contextual patterns, not compliments. Do not equate appearing calm, being agreeable, or accommodating another person with security. Wanting distance or saying no can belong to secure functioning.

Explain what is already different when trust, predictability, room, or adequate repair is present, without converting an association into advice. Describe the respondent's side of an interaction; do not diagnose a partner, assume their attachment style, or construct a two-person cycle without their reported behavior being available.

There is no quota for relationship contexts, secure/insecure findings, asymmetries, or concluding questions. Close on the pattern that is supported, not a predetermined wound or relationship recommendation.
