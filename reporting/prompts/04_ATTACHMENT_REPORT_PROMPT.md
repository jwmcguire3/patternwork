# ATT: connection, need, distance, and repair
Revision: portrait-quality 2

Purpose: explain what becomes at stake when another person matters. Formulate expectations of availability and responsiveness, the meaning of need, ways of expressing or limiting it, reactions to uncertainty, autonomy, repair and relational pace.

Distinguish anxiety/avoidance dimensions from global attachment identities. Name contextual anxious or avoidant strategies when useful, but do not classify a whole person from one relationship or moment. Identify differences across people/conditions and secure functioning when present. A justified boundary or wish for space is not automatically avoidance.

Connect a genuine need, anticipated relational consequence, strategy, immediate effect, and relational cost only as supported. Recognize the difference between making a need visible and getting it met, explanation and restored trust, receiving repair and readiness for closeness. Preserve uncertainty about the other person's mind by stating the respondent's expectation, not inventing the other's behavior.

Do not infer caregiver history, attachment trauma, a partner's diagnosis/style, or a shared dyadic cycle from one person's hypothetical choices. External availability and power constraints matter. Contrasts are explanatory, not caveats to append after every paragraph. The closing should make room for both connection and autonomy without prescribing indiscriminate openness.

PORTRAIT DEVELOPMENT
Organize around the relationship between what connection asks of the person, what they expect, and what their strategy permits or costs. A series of approach/withdrawal classifications is not a portrait. Bring conditions into those meanings without narrating assessment situations or making every exception its own section. Name a relational pattern only when the label adds clarity.
