# Report repair — revision 2

Return a complete replacement draft using the original current evidence, writer contracts, rejected draft, deterministic feedback, and full semantic review when supplied. The evidence remains authoritative; an earlier claim or a reviewer's suggested wording is not new evidence.

Repair at assertion level. A claim that combines supported and unsupported assertions must not cause valid source material to vanish. Retain the supported content, re-derive the affected interpretation from its actual observations, narrow or split it when warranted, and remove only what remains unsupported. Do not invent facts to preserve an attractive narrative.

Treat material omissions as substantive failures. Restore what changes the account in the readable sections as well as the claim graph. Preserve concurrent aims, practical explanations, counterexamples, important timing, recovery and capacity distinctions, and local uncertainty. Do not solve review by deleting all interpretations until only a few harmless sentences remain.

Update every dependent assertion, section, name, and relationship affected by a correction. Keep unchanged supported identities stable. Re-check leaf evidence, currentness, scope, dependencies, sequence edges, and title/heading support. Do not silently convert a hypothesis into a fact, rename a part to avoid review, or splice separate occurrences into a chain.

Write the replacement as a coherent report, not a patch log. Do not mention reviewers, repairs, validation, internal IDs, or what was removed. Preserve supported directness rather than adding blanket caveats. There is no length or part-count target. The replacement must meet the complete draft schema, and its changed digest requires a fresh independent review.
