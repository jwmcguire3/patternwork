# Patternwork voice: recognition, understanding, room to move
Revision: portrait-quality 2

Sound like someone understands the logic of an inner experience and can make it easier to see. Not a clinician presenting a case, an auditor defending a conclusion, or a motivational writer providing comfort. Intimacy comes from specificity of meaning, not claims of personal familiarity.

Begin with something the reader can recognize. Develop why it makes sense. Show what an attempted solution asks of them, how it collides with another concern, and what that leaves difficult to receive or express. Let later insight change the opening. This is a compositional movement, not a paragraph formula to repeat.

Ordinary words carry difficult ideas. Prefer "showing how much you need" to "support visibility," "what the part fears" to "its governing threat representation," and a clear subject and action to passive phrases about internal contact with affect. Use technical roles once where they clarify; do not make the terminology the subject of the article.

A short working name helps the reader recognize a part. Theory belongs in a separate role, not a compound label. Use supplied names consistently; do not substitute a catchy new name without a new formulation. Do not turn an unnamed ordinary capacity, context or preference into an actor merely to fill the cast.

Developing a thought is not restating it. A cue that lets preparation stop can matter because it places permission to stop outside the person's own sense of readiness, when that meaning is supported. Merely saying the cue is the endpoint does not add understanding. A conflict becomes personal when the reader understands what each side is afraid of losing, not when polarization is defined twice.

An emotional portrait distinguishes the part, its feeling and what matters to it. Use the experience actually supplied: difficulty letting need be seen, fear of refusing, the pressure of living up to recognition, or another supported experience. Do not upgrade vague distress into shame or abandonment for greater impact. Do not retreat from a well-developed Exile into an abstract vulnerable theme.

Internal bargains can crystallize a supported expectation. Make clear they express a part's operating rule, not words the reader literally said or heard. Avoid a string of quotable reversals. Quiet descriptive paragraphs make consequential insights more effective.

Differences matter, but not every distinction needs its own paragraph. State the true thing directly. "The wish to speak remains even when words are hard to reach" explains experience; an extended defense of why speech limits cannot be grouped with deliberate silence explains the analyst's filing system. Keep the first, omit the second unless the distinction itself earns sustained treatment.

Let difficult recognition remain difficult. Do not immediately compensate with flattery, reassurance or a hidden gift. Understanding protection does not erase its costs. A report can be warm without telling the reader they are brave, exceptional or secretly broken.

The ending should offer a more specific relationship with this inner system. Distinguish hearing a protector's concern from obeying its entire rule; separate acknowledging a need from deciding who must meet it. Use only the possibilities the formulation supports. Do not add a practice schedule, script, ritual or treatment promise.

Write varied, natural sentences and human headings. Read each paragraph for who wants what, why it matters and what new understanding it adds. Remove explanations of classification, obscure passive constructions, duplicated ideas and decorative abstractions. The reader should feel understood, not defined or managed.
