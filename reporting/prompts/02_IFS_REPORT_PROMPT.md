# IFS formulation: parts, their relationships, burdens and Self
Revision: portrait-quality 2

The product is an inner portrait, not a taxonomy of coping behaviors. Formulate how protection makes sense, what it costs, and where different parts' wishes cannot all be satisfied on their present terms. No fixed cast, part count or mandatory Exile count.

Managers organize prevention or continuing control. Firefighters seek relief from activated distress; dramatic behavior is not required. Timing alone does not decide the role. A critical response after an error can pursue prevention, while postponing a future demand can offer immediate relief. Use a broader role when it better represents the actual function. Do not narrate uncertainty about classifications.

Group by job, expectation, feared outcome and relationship. A coherent role can encompass more than one behavior. Similar behavior or broad relief aims do not by themselves establish the same part. A collection of interpersonal actions with different purposes is not automatically a named part called Relational Caution. Keep an ordinary need or capacity distinct from an inferred actor.

Name every retained part with a short, familiar working label. Usually one to three ordinary words work: Preparer, Critic, Peacekeeper, Watcher, or a similarly clear label chosen for this evidence. These are naming examples, not a required cast. Put manager, firefighter, Exile and other model roles in role. Do not manufacture diminutives, child identities or technical labels to make a part seem vivid.

For central parts, develop what they expect would happen if they relaxed; what makes their job necessary; what becomes harder under their rule; and any supported or clearly prospective condition that changes the demand. Distinguish a wish from a fear, a preventive expectation from a measured outcome, and an actual stopping cue from a proposed new relationship with the part. Use portraits to reference these meanings. Missing facets may stay empty, but do not mistake an underdeveloped interpretation for a lack of evidence without examining the whole pattern.

Exiles are important. Examine internally kept-away painful experience and the protective relationships around it. Infer and name an Exile when earned by the present material, without requiring a known age, a trauma memory or the respondent's own IFS vocabulary. Do not turn ordinary attentional rest, loss of capacity, one act of distraction, or concealment from someone else automatically into internal exile. Examine which feeling, belief or need is difficult to allow internally and what protection does about it. Describe an earned Exile's particular present experience rather than explaining its eligibility for the category. Never invent a burden or origin to finish the portrait.

Relationships must do explanatory work. For a polarization, identify what each side preserves, the cost each sees in the other's solution, and why neither simple surrender resolves the conflict. A conceptual relationship is not a witnessed temporal cascade. Protectors may intensify another part's difficulty without evidence they take over in a fixed order.

Do not personify unavailable words, fatigue or a capacity limit as an intentional refusal by default. Real constraints belong in the account without becoming the entire account. Protectors can answer actual demands and still add an internal cost.

Self-led possibility is a different relationship with these parts, not a superior manager or forced openness. Develop what becomes separable: recognizing a need versus obligating another person, hearing a fear versus letting it decide, taking responsibility versus self-attack. Use only distinctions supported by this formulation. No memory excavation, exposure instructions or unburdening protocol.
